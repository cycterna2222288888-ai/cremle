Biography
1965–1991
Journalism education
Born in Moscow. Studies journalism at Moscow State University. Begins her television career on the cusp of the Soviet collapse.
1991
Channel One
Joins Channel One (then Central Television of the USSR) as a news anchor. Witnesses and reads the announcements of the August 1991 coup attempt live on air.
1991–2014
Three presidents, one anchor
Andreyeva reads the evening news through the entire post-Soviet period — under Gorbachev, Yeltsin, and three Putin terms. Her consistent presence gives the state narrative an air of continuity and authority.
2022
The invasion — and sanctions
On February 24, Andreyeva reads the official announcement of the "special military operation" in her characteristic measured tone. She never uses the word "war." EU and UK sanction her for systematic state propaganda.
2024–2025
Still at the desk
Thirty-four years after joining Channel One, Andreyeva continues to read the evening news. The war continues. The word "war" remains unspoken.

The Method
Neutral voice as an instrument of legitimization. An anchor who has read state news under Gorbachev, Yeltsin, and Putin — without ever using the word "war." The illusion of objectivity, perfected over three decades. The word "war" remains legally prohibited on Russian television.
