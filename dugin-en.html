Alexander Dugin

Philosopher, founder of the Eurasian Movement, "Foundations of Geopolitics" (1997)
Born: January 7, 1962 — Moscow

Biography
1962–1990
Dissident fringe
Born in Moscow. Gravitates toward esoteric nationalist circles in the late Soviet period. Studies philosophy, reads widely in European fascist and traditionalist thought (Evola, Guénon, Schmitt). Becomes a founding figure of the National Bolshevik Party with Eduard Limonov.
1993–2000
National Bolsheviks
Co-founds the National Bolshevik Party. The movement is fringe — but its aesthetics (black-hammer-and-sickle flag, shock tactics) attract media attention. Dugin separates and moves toward a more intellectual positioning.
1997
"Foundations of Geopolitics"
Publishes his landmark work, which argues that Russia must rebuild a Eurasian empire, fragment the United States by supporting separatism, and absorb Ukraine as an existential necessity. The book becomes assigned reading in Russian military and intelligence academies.
2001
Eurasian Movement
Founds the International Eurasian Movement. Gains access to Kremlin circles. His ideas — stripped of their esoteric origins — enter mainstream state ideology under the name "Eurasianism."
2014–2022
Donbas and sanctions
Among the first prominent figures to call for Russian military intervention in eastern Ukraine. Sanctioned by the USA in 2015, by the EU after the 2022 invasion.
2022
Daughter killed
On August 20, 2022, his daughter Darya Dugina is killed when a car bomb detonates under her vehicle near Moscow. Dugin was reportedly the intended target. He continues his public activity.
2024–2025
Ideological fixture
Remains one of the most cited ideological sources for Russian state positioning. His influence is diffuse — in official speeches, in military doctrine, in the rhetoric of television hosts who have never read him.

2022
The Method
Academic language in service of imperialism. "Foundations of Geopolitics" (1997) became a textbook in Russian military academies and shaped a generation of officers and officials. Dugin does not appear on nightly television — he provides the ideological architecture that others broadcast.
