Leonid
Slutsky

Diplomacy as cover. Peace as narrative.

01

Biography

Leonid Slutsky — LDPR leader and State Duma International Affairs Committee chairman. The diplomat who led Russia's "peace negotiations" delegation in February–March 2022 while Russian forces shelled Ukrainian cities. Accused of serial harassment by multiple journalists. Sanctioned by EU, US, and UK.

1968

Born in Moscow

January 4, 1968. Graduated from the Moscow Finance Institute (now Financial University). Entered politics through the Liberal Democratic Party of Russia (LDPR), Vladimir Zhirinovsky's nationalist party. Fact

1993–2022

State Duma deputy

Elected to the State Duma in 1993. Re-elected through every subsequent cycle — nearly 30 years of uninterrupted service under LDPR. Chairs the International Affairs Committee from 2016, giving him formal access to foreign diplomatic contacts and international delegations. Fact

2018

Sexual harassment scandal

BBC Russian Service, RFE/RL, and Dozhd TV journalists report serial sexual harassment by Slutsky. Three journalists — including BBC's Farida Rustamova — go on record. The State Duma Ethics Committee clears him after a closed hearing. BBC, Deutsche Welle, and RFE/RL withdraw accreditation from the Duma in protest. Fact

Feb–Mar 2022

Head of the negotiation delegation

Leads Russia's delegation in three rounds of talks with Ukraine: Gomel (Feb 28), Brest region (Mar 3), and Belovezhskaya Pushcha (Mar 7). Declares "significant progress" while shelling of Kharkiv, Mariupol, and Kyiv suburbs continues. A humanitarian corridors agreement is reached — repeatedly violated by Russian forces. Interp.

Apr–May 2022

LDPR leader

After Zhirinovsky's death (April 6, 2022), becomes LDPR leader. Maintains the party's systemic role: theatrical nationalism providing the Kremlin with a permanent "loyal opposition" and a vehicle for extreme statements the government can later disavow. Interp.

2022–2025

Sanctions and continued role

EU, US, and UK sanctions imposed 2022 for "support for actions undermining Ukraine's territorial integrity." Continues as LDPR leader and International Affairs Committee chairman. Meets with foreign officials in non-sanctioning countries. Fact

03

Method

Slutsky is Russia's "diplomatic face": his negotiation role in February–March 2022 served not to produce peace but to demonstrate good faith to international audiences while military operations continued. The talks were a holding action — generating headlines about "Russian willingness to negotiate" while forces regrouped after the failed Kyiv offensive. Interp.

As LDPR leader he inherited Zhirinovsky's function: performing extreme nationalism to define the boundaries of acceptable discourse and provide cover for the government's own positions. Whatever the Kremlin does, LDPR has already said something more radical — making government policy seem moderate by comparison. Slutsky maintains this role with less flamboyance than Zhirinovsky but equivalent structural utility. Interp.

As International Affairs Committee chairman he controls parliamentary-level diplomatic contacts — meetings with foreign politicians that provide partial international legitimacy without the full weight of Foreign Ministry engagement. Visiting delegations meeting Slutsky can claim to have "engaged with Russia" without having met Lavrov or Putin. Interp.

04

Sanctions

EU — personal sanctions since 2022 (Council Regulation EU 2022/330). Ground: "as member of the State Duma voted in favour of the statement requesting recognition of the non-government controlled areas of the Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts of Ukraine as independent entities." Asset freeze, travel ban. Fact

USA — OFAC designation (Executive Order 14024). Full asset freeze in US jurisdiction. Fact

UK — Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019. Asset freeze, travel ban. Fact
